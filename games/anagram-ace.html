How to Play Anagram Ace

Anagram Ace hands you seven scrambled letter tiles, drawn from a hidden seed word, and ninety seconds to pull as many smaller words as you can. Type a word on the keyboard or tap the tiles in order - every tap lights a letter into the answer strip - then press Enter or the ✓ Submit button to lock it in. Tap any tile you have already placed to put it back, press Backspace to erase the last typed letter, and use the 🔄 Shuffle button whenever the letters feel stuck: re-scrambling the row costs nothing.

A word counts when it is at least three letters long, never reuses a tile and exists in the game's dictionary. Anything else shakes the answer strip and pops a short toast — "not in list" for unknown words, or a tile warning when you need letters you do not have. Wrong guesses also break your streak multiplier, so guess with care.

Scoring grows with length. Three letters score 30 points, four score 60, five score 100, six score 160 and the full seven-letter seed scores 250. Land three valid words in a row and everything scores ×2; six in a row lifts it to ×3. Your found words appear below the tiles, grouped by length, so you can see which sizes are still worth chasing. When the 90-second clock expires the results card shows your score, best score, words found and best word — then "Next round" deals a fresh seed word. Your best score is saved in this browser.

Controls

  • A–Z type letters, Backspace erases, Enter submits
  • Tap tiles to build a word; tap a placed tile to return it to the row
  • 🔄 Shuffle re-scrambles the tiles, ✓ Submit and ⌫ Clear sit under the board
  • Space starts a round, P or Esc pauses (tap the P tile if a word must start with it), and the Reset button restarts at any time

Tips for a high score

  • Bank a few three-letter words early to switch the multiplier on before big finds.
  • Shuffle often — a fresh order makes hidden words leap into view.
  • Leave the seven-letter seed until last; it is worth 250 before any multiplier.
  • Never guess unsure words: a broken streak costs more than a skipped word.
